MINIMALLY INVASIVE GLAUCOMA SURGERY

What is MIGS?

Glaucoma can damage the optic nerve slowly as pressure builds inside the eye. MIGS is a group of procedures designed to lower eye pressure through a small internal approach.

MIGS works from inside the eye, uses the eye's natural drainage pathway and generally leaves the outer wall of the eye untouched. That can preserve future treatment options.

Before MIGS, the choices were often daily glaucoma drops for life or a major glaucoma operation, with little in between. MIGS provides a less invasive middle option for selected patients, designed to lower eye pressure while preserving the eye's natural drainage pathway.

One of the important advantages of MIGS is its high safety margin. Because the procedures are designed to work from inside the eye while preserving the outer wall, they can offer a measured approach for appropriately selected patients.

Inside the eye Accesses the drainage angle through a micro-incision.
Preserves anatomy Conjunctiva and sclera remain intact.
Quick recovery Many patients return to routine activity within days.

HOW IT WORKS

How MIGS lowers eye pressure

In many open-angle glaucomas, the bottleneck is the trabecular meshwork. MIGS targets that point in three ways.

01

Bypass

Creates a pathway through the trabecular meshwork so fluid can reach Schlemm's canal.

02

Restore

Uses the eye's natural drainage route rather than an external bleb.

03

Maintain

Some implants also scaffold the canal to keep outflow open over time.

THE IMPLANTS

Two implants, one goal

The right device depends on drainage anatomy, target pressure and whether cataract surgery is being performed at the same time.

iStent inject® W

Two tiny titanium stents

Each stent is about 360 microns across. They are delivered through one incision in one pass, with multiple outlets to support aqueous outflow.

  • Two stents, one pass, one incision
  • 360 microns across
  • Implant-grade titanium, heparin-coated
  • Multi-directional outflow
Hydrus® Microstent

A flexible canal scaffold

About 8 mm long, the nitinol scaffold curves inside Schlemm's canal and spans approximately 90° to support outflow along its length.

Hydrus® is the only implant that both bypasses the trabecular meshwork and holds Schlemm's canal open.

  • Spans about 90° of Schlemm's canal
  • Flexible nitinol design
  • Scaffolds the canal open
  • Reaches collector channels
Both implants are used for selected mild-to-moderate open-angle glaucoma. Device choice is made after direct examination of the drainage angle.

SUITABILITY

Is MIGS right for you?

MIGS is not for every patient. A direct examination is more useful than a checklist alone.

MIGS may suit you if

  • Mild-to-moderate open-angle glaucoma
  • Pressure remains uncontrolled on drops alone
  • Drops cause burning, redness, allergy or are difficult to maintain
  • Cataract surgery is planned and pressure can be treated at the same sitting
  • Drainage angle is open and healthy on gonioscopy
  • Medication-controlled pressure is up to roughly 30 mmHg

MIGS may not be the right choice if

  • Advanced glaucoma needs a very low target pressure
  • Angle-closure or neovascular glaucoma is present
  • The drainage angle is scarred or structurally abnormal
  • Pressure is influenced by raised venous pressure around the eye
The key question is suitability. Gonioscopy and OCT imaging help Dr. Cyres decide whether MIGS is appropriate - or whether another treatment would serve you better.
WHAT HAPPENS

The procedure, step by step

Assessment, precise angle viewing and implant placement are planned around your anatomy.

01

Assessment

Pressure readings, gonioscopy, OCT and visual-field testing.

02

On the day

Local anaesthetic. You are awake and comfortable, with no overnight stay.

03

Micro-incision

About 1.5 mm at the edge of the cornea, designed to seal itself.

04

View the angle

A gonioscopy lens provides a magnified view of the drainage angle.

05

Place the implant

The microstent is guided through the trabecular meshwork into the drainage pathway.

06

Confirm position

Placement is checked directly under the microscope.

07

Close

The incision seals itself; stitches are usually not needed.

08

Go home

Short recovery, then home the same day.

On its own, the procedure usually takes about 15 to 20 minutes. When it is combined with cataract surgery, it adds only a few minutes to the operation you were already having.

AFTERCARE

What recovery looks like

Recovery varies by patient and procedure. Follow your individual post-operative plan.

Day 1-3

Some grittiness and blur can be normal. Use drops as prescribed.

Week 1

Vision settles, follow-up begins and light routine usually resumes.

Week 2-4

Most normal activity returns as pressure is reviewed and medicines are adjusted.

Month 2-3

Pressure stabilises and the longer-term medication plan becomes clearer.

Looking after the eye

  • Use prescribed drops exactly as scheduled.
  • Do not rub the eye for at least four weeks.
  • Avoid swimming, heavy lifting and strenuous exercise for two to four weeks.
  • Keep the eye clean and avoid dust and smoke where possible.
  • Attend the planned follow-up visits: Day 1, Week 1, Month 1 and Month 3.
  • Call the clinic the same day for increasing pain, sudden blurring or discharge.
THE EVIDENCE

What the evidence shows

These figures come from published multi-centre clinical trials, not from our own case series.

7.0 mmHg mean unmedicated IOP reduction at 24 months with iStent inject® in a 505-patient randomised trial.
59% of Hydrus® patients were medication-free at five years in long-term HORIZON data.
55% relative reduction in further glaucoma surgery at five years with Hydrus® (2.4% vs 5.3%).
Comparable serious adverse-event rates at five years compared with cataract surgery alone.
Important: Trial averages do not predict an individual result. MIGS can lower pressure and reduce medication burden, but it does not restore vision already lost and it is not a cure for glaucoma.
